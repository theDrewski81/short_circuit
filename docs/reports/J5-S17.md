## 4. Decisions taken, with rationale

**Hand-authored SVG, not KiCad.** As the brief directed, and for the reason it gave: this is
an interconnect drawing whose consumers are a bench and a parts order, and the repository
already has a proven diffable convention for exactly that.

**Passives are properties of a net, not endpoints of their own, and the BOM now carries
them.** A series resistor or a bulk capacitor does not terminate a conductor, so modelling
them as endpoints would have forced an exemption list into check 4 and weakened it. Andrew
approved adding the resistor line to `BOM.md`, so the values are now orderable and check 5
asserts that every value called out on a net is a `BOM.md` line item. Without that line the
harness could have been drawn, approved and ordered against with five components missing.

**Rail names carry in the net name, and every rail-prefixed net must declare a current.**
`VBAT_`, `5V_`, `6V_`, `3V3M_` and `3V3V_` are rails; `SIG_`, `OUT_` and `GND_` are not, and a
net with any other prefix fails. A rail-prefixed net must declare either `[peak N A]` or
`[reflected]`. Mutation 3a is what that rule buys: a forgotten figure fails loudly rather than
summing to zero.

**Inter-rail feeds are `[reflected]`, not peaks.** The two buck inputs draw real current, but
charging it to VBAT as well as to the rail it supplies would double-count it and make the net
table irreconcilable with the Power Budget, which models rails as loads. The reflected current
is instead computed once, in the fuse check, from each rail's total.

**The Power Budget's "3.3 V / 5 V" sensors row is charged to the first rail named.** Splitting
it across both would add its 0.05 A to the 5 V rail as well and make a correct net table read
as 0.05 A short. The rule is stated in the document and implemented in one place in the guard.

**The net table may exceed the Power Budget by up to 0.25 A per rail, and may never fall below
it.** The table is finer-grained than the budget: it pays for the encoder supplies and the
TB6612 logic supply that the budget rolls into other rows. A missing load makes the table fall
short and fails; an invented one blows the allowance and fails.

**Two derived 3.3 V supplies, taken from the Pi header pins rather than a third converter.**
Pi-M's pin carries the I²C sensors, both motor encoder supplies and TB6612 VCC, which is what
keeps every logic and encoder output GPIO-safe and is what the bench schematic already
established. Pi-V's carries the microphone. Their combined 0.09 A is checked against a
declared 0.25 A ceiling for a Pi Zero 2 W 3.3 V pin, which is an assumption of mine and is
labelled as such in both the document and the guard, because `BOM.md` states no such rating.

**The battery sense divider is 100 kΩ over 47 kΩ, downstream of the master switch.** 8.4 V
divides to 2.685 V, inside both the 3.3 V VDD and the ±4.096 V full-scale range, at 57 µA.
Placing it after the switch means telemetry reads zero when the robot is off, which is the
correct behaviour rather than a defect: the alternative leaves a permanent load on a
disconnected-looking pack.

**Encoder returns go to Pi-M, not to the star.** The encoder return follows its own signal
pair back to the Pi in the same six-conductor cable. Sending it to the star would put a
signal return in parallel with a motor return.

**Ground is drawn as a bus and wired as a star.** This is the bench drawing's own convention
and it is labelled on the drawing so nobody builds a daisy chain from it. Upper-band blocks
use ground symbols rather than routed lines, for the blocks where a routed return would have
to cross most of the drawing to say the same thing.

**Two checks beyond the four the brief required.** Check 5 asserts passive values are BOM line
items, which is what justifies not exempting them from check 4. Check 6 asserts the drawing
and the document carry the same 64 net names, because two files describing one harness drifting
apart is the most likely way this deliverable rots.

## 7. Open threads discovered

**The 7.5 A fuse has an 11 % margin.** Worst-case simultaneous draw at the fuse is 6.65 A
against 7.5 A: 2.00 A direct on the motor rail, 1.95 A reflected through the 5 V buck and
2.70 A through the 6 V buck, at 7.4 V nominal and an assumed 90 % efficiency. It is a
synthetic number, because a motor stall and a servo stall and speech at full volume do not
coincide, but it is the tightest margin in the power architecture and it should be a decision
rather than a discovery. Two things bear on it. The fuse must be a blade type and not a glass
fast-blow, because three bulk capacitors charging at switch-on is real inrush. And if the
deferred motor-rail boost to 9–10 V is ever taken up, the calculation has to be redone before
it is wired. Recommend deciding this before the order goes in, not after.

## 9. Answered by Andrew after this report was written

Recorded here rather than folded into the sections above, so that what the session found and
what Andrew ruled stay distinguishable. All three were settled on 2026-08-25, after section 8
was written.

**The #5218 / #5219 contradiction was a deliberate fit test, and it is over.** One of each
variant was bought so the fit could be tested with the parts in hand. **The #5219, the
side-connector variant, does not fit the chassis cleanly. Both drive motors are #5218, and
#5218 is the only drive motor this project will use.** The two part numbers differ only in the
orientation of the wiring connection. That confirms `BOM.md`, and it changes the character of
the remaining references rather than merely resolving a disagreement: the #5219 in
`docs/HARDWARE_drive_bringup.md`, in `docs/diagrams/bench_full_schematic.svg` and in
`simulation/chassis/TUNING.md` line 72 is now plainly wrong. Correcting it is a mechanical
pass with no open question left in it, and it needs nothing further from the bench.
`TUNING.md` needs the most care of the three, because its "side assignment locked" line
carries two separate facts: the part numbers, which were wrong, and the left and right channel
assignment with its lead and encoder crossovers, which was measured and is right. Only the
part numbers should go.

**The 6-pin JST SH encoder cables ship with the motors and need no `BOM.md` line.** Recording
the connector type in the net table's Connector column and on the drawing is enough to
identify a replacement if one is ever needed. The thread in section 7 is closed, not deferred.
The sentence this session added to `BOM.md` section 1 saying the cables are "sold separately,
and are not yet a line item here" was wrong, and is corrected in the same commit as this
addendum.

**The fuse was informational, and section 7 overstated it by calling it a decision.** The
7.5 A inline blade fuse already specified in `BOM.md` section 6 stands, unchanged. Three
things settle it. The 6.65 A figure is a synthetic all-peak sum that no operating point
reaches; a realistic worst case is a motor stall plus one servo movement plus audio, roughly
3.5 to 4 A. A blade fuse carries its rating continuously and needs roughly twice it to open in
seconds, so even a sustained 6.65 A would not open a 7.5 A blade. And the switch-on inrush is
about 0.1 J across all three bulk electrolytics, orders of magnitude below the fuse's I²t,
which makes it a question about master-switch contact life rather than about the fuse, and not
much of one at that energy. A larger fuse would only weaken protection against a partial short
for nothing gained.

Two things from that analysis survive as standing cautions rather than decisions. Do not
substitute a fast-blow glass fuse into the same holder. And if the deferred motor-rail boost
to 9-10 V is ever taken up, the battery-side calculation in section 5 of
`docs/HARDWARE_power_harness.md` has to be redone before it is wired.

**What this leaves.** Of the six threads in section 7, two are closed outright and one is
downgraded to a standing caution. Three remain open: the superseded 1655 g mass claim in
`BOM.md`, the 1807 counts-per-revolution figure in `BOM.md` section 1, and the four-commit
staleness of `PROJECT_STATE.md`. The correction pass over the three #5219 references joins
them, now unblocked. **Nothing gates ordering any more.**
